# Role context

Describe the role before comparing the candidate. Preserve JD references and claim status; Unknown is a useful result.

Industry is the business sector of the actual role/client, not its technology, job family or vendor arrangement. “AI”, “data analyst” and “client site” alone establish no industry. Describe business scenario by the stated users, data, workflow and problem; distinguish known task context from unprovided business context. Do not research the company here.

| Work nature | Required basis | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Execution contribution | Produces analyses, implementations or other deliverables | May also build methods; does not mean junior or no discretion |
| Delivery coordination | Organizes scope, milestones, acceptance and stakeholders | Coordination does not establish product roadmap ownership |
| Domain owner | Owns decisions and outcomes in a defined domain | “Responsible for” alone proves neither authority nor final accountability |
| Overall product or business owner | Owns broad priorities/roadmap and product/business outcomes | A product-manager title is insufficient |
| Mixed | Multiple evidenced types materially coexist; name them | Framework construction plus execution does not automatically prove ownership |
| Unknown | Duties/authority do not support a defensible type | Missing management language is not explicit absence |

Record what the role decides and what outcomes/deliverables it answers for. Owner types require source-backed decision rights and accountability. Known type is usually Supported Inference; quotes describe the posting, not verified employment reality. Title-only citations cannot establish work nature.

People management is independent: Explicit yes or Explicit no requires explicit JD wording; otherwise Unknown. Cross-team collaboration, “lead a project” and stakeholder coordination do not establish direct reports or appraisal authority. These types are not a seniority or desirability ranking.
